Retire on purpose, not by accident.

Prepare early. Let time and interest do the heavy lifting.

Your CPF (Central Provident Fund) is the biggest retirement account most Singaporeans will ever own. Yet many people only open their statement when something forces them to. This guide shows you, in plain words, how to make every CPF dollar work harder for you, whether you are 30, 50 or 70.

Three neighbours. Same birthday. Very different retirements.

This is an illustrative story with made-up people. The CPF figures in it are the official 2026 figures.

Mdm Lim, Mr Tan and Mr Ong live on the same floor and all three turn 55 in 2026. Same age, the same salary of $5,000 a month, similar lives. The difference is a habit.

Years ago, Mdm Lim started opening her CPF statement once a year. She learned what each account was for, kept her MediSave healthy, and topped up her own CPF when she could. Mr Tan said, "CPF will sort itself out," and never looked. Mr Ong went one step further than Mdm Lim: he prepared early. For years he set money aside on purpose, so that at 55 he could top up his Retirement Account to the Enhanced Retirement Sum without a scramble.

On their 55th birthday, CPF sets up a Retirement Account (RA) for each of them, and their Special Account (SA) closes. What counts now is how much sits in the RA, because that money buys a monthly income for life through CPF LIFE (Lifelong Income For the Elderly).

Compare the three, every month for life (estimates):

Mdm Lim gets about $830 more than Mr Tan, roughly $9,960 a year.

Mr Ong gets about $1,660 more than Mdm Lim, roughly $19,920 a year.

Mr Ong gets about $2,490 more than Mr Tan, roughly $29,880 a year, which is more than three times Mr Tan's monthly income.

The extra is not luck. It is how much each of them chose to set aside at 55. The rest of this guide shows how they did it.

The trade-off for Mr Ong: to reach the Enhanced Retirement Sum he had to set aside $220,400 more than Mdm Lim did, from his CPF savings or his own cash. That money is locked in for retirement payouts and cannot be taken back, so it only makes sense if he will not need it for other things. Whether it is right depends on your own situation.

The quiet hero: compounding interest

Between 55 and 65, none of the three adds a cent. Their Retirement Accounts simply sit and earn the guaranteed 4% a year. Each year's interest is added to the balance, so next year they earn interest on the interest too. That is compounding, and it is why the gap keeps widening.

Of which, interest on interest$8,843$17,686$35,372
  • Mr Ong's interest alone is about $211,700. That is nearly double the entire amount Mr Tan set aside, earned just by waiting.
  • Interest on interest is real money. Plain (non-compounding) interest of 4% for 10 years would give Mr Ong $176,320. Compounding gives him $35,372 more, for doing nothing extra.
  • Double the sum, double the interest. Each person sets aside twice what the one before did, so each earns twice as much interest.
  • Time is the other ingredient. The earlier money goes in, the more years it has to compound. That is why Ethan's story below starts at 22, not 55.

How we worked this out: sum set aside at 55 × 1.04 for each year, with no withdrawals and nothing added. 4% a year is the lowest rate CPF currently guarantees on the Retirement Account and is reviewed every three months, so real interest could be higher. The extra interest on the first $60,000 of combined balances (2% on the first $30,000 and 1% on the next $30,000 for members 55 and above) is left out, so the true figures could be higher still. This is a simple illustration, not a forecast of your own account.

Payouts are estimates for people turning 55 in 2026, as reported by SmartWealth and other sources listed at the bottom. Your own figure depends on your age and what you set aside. Check the CPF Board's official tools for your exact number.

Prepare early: the difference between a scramble and a plan

Mr Ong needed $220,400 more than Mdm Lim at 55 to reach the Enhanced Retirement Sum. Finding that sum in one go at 55 is hard. Building it slowly, from early on, is far easier, and compounding does a large share of the work.

  • Start at 55: your money has about 10 years to compound before payouts. In the examples above, interest is about a third of the final balance.
  • Start at 22: your money has 43 years to compound. In Ethan's example, interest is about two-thirds of the final balance.
  • Less from your own pocket: in Ethan's example, about $70,100 of his own cash, spread over his career, was enough to fill his Retirement Account to the Enhanced Retirement Sum on his 55th birthday.
  • Every year you wait has a cost, because that year's compounding cannot be won back later.

Whatever your age, the best time to start is now. Open your CPF statement this week, then read Ethan's story below.

The second story: Ethan uses every lever, from his first pay cheque to 65

Another made-up person, with a worked example. The rules and rates are the 2026 ones; the result is a projection, not a promise.

Ethan starts his first job at 22 on $5,000 a month. Nothing fancy. In this example he never gets a pay rise, so at 55 he earns exactly the same $5,000 a month as Mdm Lim, Mr Tan and Mr Ong in the first story. His friend, "Default Dan", earns the same and does only what is automatic: his employer and he pay CPF every month, and that is it. Ethan decides to use every CPF option open to him, every year, for 43 years.

Ethan's toolkit

1. Cash top-up to his Special Account

$8,000 a year under the Retirement Sum Topping-Up Scheme (RSTU), the amount that qualifies for income tax relief.

2. Move his Ordinary Account savings to his Special Account

Every year he transfers his OA savings into the SA (4% a year, instead of 2.5% in the OA), up to the Full Retirement Sum.

3. Fill the Retirement Account to the Enhanced Retirement Sum

At 55 he tops up his new Retirement Account with OA savings and cash, up to $440,800 (the 2026 Enhanced Retirement Sum).

4. Keep topping up after 55

If there is room under the limit, he adds more each year until 65.

Also available, but not counted in his Retirement Account

MediSave top-ups (they share the same $8,000 tax relief cap), and top-ups to his parents' accounts (another $8,000 of tax relief).

Ethan's road to 65

  • Age 22. First job. He starts the cash top-ups and yearly OA-to-SA transfers straight away.
  • About age 30. His Special Account reaches the Full Retirement Sum ($220,400 in 2026 terms). Interest keeps growing it at 4% a year.
  • Age 55, on his birthday. His Special Account closes. His savings move into a new Retirement Account up to the Full Retirement Sum, and he tops it up straight away to the Enhanced Retirement Sum: $440,800. This is exactly where Mr Ong is at 55.
  • Age 55 to 65. No more room to add, so interest does the work at 4% a year.
  • Age 65. His Retirement Account is worth about $652,000, the same as Mr Ong's.
Default Dan: automatic contributions onlyabout $326,000
Ethan: every lever, every yearabout $652,000

Same salary. Same job. Ethan ends up with about $326,000 more in his Retirement Account at 65, exactly double Dan's.

And in this example, the only cash he adds from his own pocket is about $70,000 over his whole career. The rest comes from moving money he already has in his CPF to where it earns more.

Why Ethan's monthly payout at 65 is the same as Mr Ong's, about $3,440: the Enhanced Retirement Sum is the most CPF lets you set aside, and both men have it in their Retirement Accounts from age 55. So their balances at 65 are the same ($652,492 in this example) and so are their estimated payouts. Ethan's advantage is not a bigger payout at 65. It is that he got there with about $70,100 of his own cash over 33 years of work, instead of finding a large sum late, and that he can still choose to wait until 70 for more (see below). Use the CPF Board's Retirement Payout Planner to see what a balance like his could pay each month.

Ethan's bonus option: wait until 70

CPF lets you start your CPF LIFE monthly payouts anywhere from 65 to 70. Each year you wait, your monthly payout can rise by up to 7%, and your Retirement Account keeps earning 4% interest while you wait. If you do nothing, payouts begin by themselves at 70.

Say Ethan keeps working or living on other savings until 70, and does not touch his Retirement Account.

  • Five more years of compounding: waiting adds about $141,400 of interest to his Retirement Account, without him adding a cent.
  • The official benchmark: for a 55-year-old man with the Full Retirement Sum ($220,400) on the CPF LIFE Standard Plan, CPF says starting at 70 gives $2,380 a month instead of $1,780 at 65, which is $600 more.
  • The catch: Ethan gives up five years of payouts, about $206,000 at the lower amount. At roughly $13,900 extra a year, he would only come out ahead if he lives to about 85 or beyond. CPF itself says deferring pays off only if you live long enough, and that he needs another source of income from 65 to 70.

Good to know: the 2027 sums are already announced: $114,100 (Basic), $228,200 (Full) and $456,400 (Enhanced). The BRS and FRS that apply to you are fixed at the year you turn 55 and stay the same for life. The Enhanced Retirement Sum is a ceiling that rises each January. These sums are a target to aim for, not an amount you are forced to save.

Your CPF in plain words: four pots of money

Think of CPF as four pots. Each pot has one job.

Ordinary Account (OA)

For housing, insurance and education. Earns 2.5% a year.

Special Account (SA)

For retirement, for members below 55. Earns 4% a year. It closes when you turn 55.

MediSave Account (MA)

For hospital bills and approved medical needs. Earns 4% a year.

Retirement Account (RA)

Created at 55. It pays for your CPF LIFE monthly income. Earns 4% a year.

The interest rates above are the official rates for 1 July to 30 September 2026. They are reviewed every three months, so check the CPF website for the current quarter.

The "bonus interest" most people forget

Below 55: you earn an extra 1% a year on the first $60,000 of your combined CPF balances (counting no more than $20,000 from your OA).

55 and above: you earn an extra 2% a year on the first $30,000 and an extra 1% on the next $30,000 (again counting no more than $20,000 from your OA). Even if you join CPF LIFE, you still earn this extra interest.

One scheme, two jobs: your lifetime safety net and your retirement paycheque

Most people think of CPF as "retirement money". It is also quietly doing a second job from your very first pay cheque: protecting you and your family when life goes wrong. Used well, the same CPF that pays you every month in old age also helps you survive a hospital bill, a lost income or a long illness without wiping out your savings.

Layer 1: When you fall ill

MediSave (your healthcare savings pot) pays for hospital stays and some long-term care, for you and your dependants.

MediShield Life is basic health insurance for large hospital bills. Every Singapore Citizen and Permanent Resident is covered automatically, and premiums are paid from MediSave.

Result: a big medical bill does not have to eat your retirement savings.

Layer 2: If you become severely disabled

CareShield Life gives monthly cash payouts for life if you become severely disabled. You are covered automatically from age 30, and premiums are payable by MediSave.

Result: long-term care costs are partly covered by money you already have.

Layer 3: If your income stops

Dependants' Protection Scheme (DPS) is basic term life insurance that applies automatically to CPF members between 21 and 65 who make CPF contributions. It pays a sum of up to $70,000 in the event of death, terminal illness or total permanent disability, to help your family through the first difficult period.

Result: your family has a cushion if you cannot earn.

Layer 4: To keep your home

The Home Protection Scheme (HPS) reduces your home loan if you die, are terminally ill or become totally and permanently disabled, so that you and your family do not lose your Housing and Development Board (HDB) flat.

Result: your family keeps the roof over their heads.

Layer 5: If you live a very long time

CPF LIFE is national longevity insurance. It turns your Retirement Account savings into a monthly payout for as long as you live, starting from 65, or later if you wait up to 70. This is the retirement paycheque in the stories above: about $950, $1,780 or $3,440 a month for people turning 55 in 2026, depending on how much they set aside.

Result: you cannot outlive your income, however long you live.

How to make CPF do both jobs well

  • Protect MediSave first. Keep it at a healthy level, because it pays your health insurance premiums for life. Check how much MediSave you have each year.
  • Keep your cover active. DPS and the Home Protection Scheme are tied to your CPF contributions and your home loan. Check your cover on the CPF website, and look at private insurance if you have dependants who rely heavily on your income, since the DPS cover is basic.
  • Grow the retirement pot while it protects you. The Special and Retirement Accounts are guaranteed to earn at least 4% a year, plus extra interest on the first $60,000 of your combined balances. That safe growth is the engine behind the stories on this page.
  • Think ahead on the trade-off. Money set aside for retirement payouts cannot be taken back for other uses. Keep other savings for emergencies, so you never need to touch your CPF for them.
  • Turn it into income on your terms. Choose your CPF LIFE start age between 65 and 70 and your plan, using the CPF Board's tools.

Cover amounts, who qualifies and the premiums depend on your age, health status and circumstances, and they can change. This is general information, not advice. Check the CPF Board and your insurer for your own position.

Your CPF Compass: 4 steps

Four moves, in order. You can start any day.

  1. Know where you stand

    Log in to the CPF website or the CPF mobile app and look at your balances in each account. Write down your age, your total, and which retirement sum you are aiming for.

  2. Let it grow

    Keep your savings working in the accounts that earn the most. Below 55, that is the Special Account at 4% plus extra interest. Check whether you can use your Ordinary Account savings to boost your retirement account, but read the CPF Board's rules first, especially if you still need that money for your home.

  3. Top up, if you can

    Cash top-ups to your own account (called Retirement Sum Topping-Up, or RSTU) can grow your retirement savings and may give you tax relief. If you are 55 or above and need a hand, the Matched Retirement Savings Scheme (MRSS) matches eligible cash top-ups dollar for dollar. Always check the eligibility rules first.

  4. Turn savings into a paycheque

    At payout age, your savings in the Retirement Account fund CPF LIFE, a government scheme that pays you every month for as long as you live. Look at the payout estimates on the CPF website before you choose how much to set aside.

Where are you on the road?

Pick your stage. The advice changes.

You have time. That is your biggest asset.

  • Open your CPF statement once a year. Ten minutes is enough.
  • Understand the bonus interest. The first $60,000 of your combined balances earns an extra 1% a year.
  • Think about topping up your Special Account in good years. Small amounts add up over time because interest earns interest.
  • Keep your MediSave Account healthy. Medical bills are one of the biggest threats to retirement savings.
  • Use a target: the Full Retirement Sum is the figure to aim for. The sum for the year you turn 55 will be higher than today's, so treat today's figure as a guide only.

Every dollar now has a clear job.

  • At 55 your Special Account closes and your savings move to the Retirement Account, up to the Full Retirement Sum. Check what has landed there.
  • Your extra interest is bigger now: 2% extra on the first $30,000 and 1% extra on the next $30,000 of your combined balances.
  • If you are still working, higher CPF contribution rates for senior workers in 2026 go into your Retirement Account, up to the Full Retirement Sum. Your take-home pay is a little lower, but your future payout is higher.
  • Compare your options: Basic, Full or Enhanced. A higher sum means a higher monthly payout for life.
  • If you have cash to spare, a top-up before payouts begin can raise your monthly income. If you have limited income, ask about the Matched Retirement Savings Scheme.

The honest truth

CPF LIFE is built to cover your basic needs for life, which is valuable. It is not designed to pay for every lifestyle wish. Many people also build extra savings outside CPF, such as through the Supplementary Retirement Scheme (SRS) or other long-term savings. The right mix depends on you, and this guide is general information, not financial advice.

Before making a big decision, check the CPF Board's official calculators and talk to the CPF Board or a licensed financial adviser.
